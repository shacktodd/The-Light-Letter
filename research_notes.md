| Historical marine observations | NOAA/NCEI describes ICOADS as a surface-marine observation collection from ships, buoys, and other platforms, spanning 1662 to the present. It distributes monthly summaries on 2° × 2° boxes back to 1800 and maintains provenance-aware input-source handling. | Documented institutional data scope; does not by itself validate a specific causal historical claim. | NOAA/NCEI, *International Comprehensive Ocean–Atmosphere Data Set (ICOADS)*, https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/international-comprehensive-ocean-atmosphere-data-set |
| Caste associations | Lucy Carroll’s abstract argues that late-nineteenth/early-twentieth-century caste organizations and polemics responded more strongly than previously recognized to foreign efforts to describe, define, interpret, and categorize Indian society. The extract explicitly calls this effect largely unintended. | Scholarly interpretation grounded in historical sources; the full article must not be overgeneralized to all regions, groups, or colonial mechanisms. | Lucy Carroll, “Colonial Perceptions of Indian Society and the Emergence of Caste(s) Associations,” *Journal of Asian Studies* 37(2), 1978, DOI: https://doi.org/10.2307/2054164 |
| Mosque astronomy | HMML documents that prominent urban mosques employed *muwaqqits* from at least the thirteenth century: astronomy specialists who set prayer times and religious calendars using instruments such as quadrants and astrolabes. It identifies Ibn al-Shāṭir (1304–1375) as a *muwaqqit* at Damascus’s Umayyad Mosque and describes manuscript treatises and tables as practical guides for religious use. | Well-documented institutional and manuscript evidence. It does not prove a single institutional motive or that all advances in mathematical astronomy originated in religious demand. | Josh Mugler, “Astronomical Technology and Religious Practice in Islam,” Hill Museum & Manuscript Library, https://hmml.org/stories/series-celestial-astronomical-technology/; David A. King, “The Astronomy of the Mamluks,” *Isis* 74(4), 1983, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1086/353360 |
| Imperial logbooks | The CLIWOC project abstracted more than 280,000 daily weather observations from British, Dutch, French, and Spanish naval logbooks for 1750–1854. The source describes the vessels as engaged in imperial business and reports reconstructions relevant to ENSO and NAO; it also says the database represented less than 10% of the known archival data volume. | Primary project publication plus NOAA archival context. Spatial routes, language conversion, archival survival, and navigation/empire biases remain material limitations. | R. García-Herrera et al., “Ship logbooks help analyze pre-instrumental climate,” *EOS* 87(18), 2006, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1029/2006EO180002; NOAA/NCEI ICOADS page above. |

## Research guardrails

The report will distinguish an archive or classification’s **documented operation** from any interpretation of its creators’ intention. Claims that a census category or shipping bureaucracy caused a later result require more than temporal coincidence; uneven collection, archival survival, and participant agency are active denominators. Source coverage, region, and date will be stated when known.
